Hello there.

I am Anmol, a software engineer working on backend systems and the platforms that carry them. I am drawn to the work that only becomes interesting at scale — where a query that is perfectly fine at ten thousand records quietly falls apart at three hundred million, and where the job is less about inventing something new than about making a system honest about what it is doing.

Much of my work has been in telemetry and observability. At Microsoft I am on the team behind service telemetry for Microsoft Fabric, and I spent four months shipping the Kusto alerting service onto the French sovereign cloud. The rest of it is migration without data loss, shaving CPU off dozens of clusters, and writing the runbook so that nobody has to rediscover a failure mode at 3am. Before that I built retrieval-augmented generation features at Clari, and autonomous 3D scanning workflows at Trimble.

I think about systems more than I think about code. The interesting part is rarely the component you are staring at — it is the feedback loops between them, the queues, the retries, the places where a small local decision quietly becomes a global problem. I like architectures that are resilient and simple, that do what they are meant to and nothing more, and I am suspicious of complexity that is still around because nobody ever removed it.

## Now

I am a Software Engineer at Microsoft, working on service telemetry and observability infrastructure. Before Microsoft I was at Clari, and before that Trimble. I studied Computer Engineering at IIIT Design and Manufacturing, where I also organised and taught a Data Science with Python workshop.

Away from a terminal, I read — mostly non-fiction, science and history — which is why there is a [bookshelf]({{ '/books/' | relative_url }}). I run, play basketball and badminton, and meditate; that last one has done more for my ability to hold on to a single thought than any tool I have installed. I remain reliably fascinated by natural systems and by the human body, and I suspect that is not entirely unrelated to the first thing.

I am curious about most of what I come across, which is probably the main reason any of this is worth doing. I keep the garage door open, and a fair amount of what I have ended up building started life as an accident.
